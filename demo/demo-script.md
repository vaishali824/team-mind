# TeamMind Demo Script

## 0:00–0:20 — Problem

“When a customer changes account owners, the context often disappears with the previous employee's session. The customer has to explain the same concern again, and a proposal can be mistaken for a promise.”

Show the customer list and the Employee A / Employee B handoff control.

## 0:20–0:45 — Employee A conversation

Select Rahul Sharma (`CUST-001`) and keep Employee A selected. Read the transcript: Rahul likes the product, thinks ₹50,000 is expensive, asks whether 20% off is possible, and says he *may* consider buying next month.

Point out the wording: “may consider” and “is any possibility” are not commitments or approvals.

## 0:45–1:15 — Store the memory

Click **Store in Hindsight**. Explain that TeamMind calls Hindsight `retain` with Rahul's customer bank, conversation ID, employee/source metadata, and a customer scope tag. For a quick setup, **Seed demo histories** performs the same real retain operation for all five fictional accounts.

## 1:15–1:30 — Handoff

Switch the account owner to Employee B. Employee B is a new persona in this demo and did not participate in the conversation. The persisted memory belongs to Rahul's account, not Employee A's browser session.

## 1:30–2:15 — Employee B asks for context

Click **Brief me**. Explain that TeamMind uses Hindsight `recall` with Rahul's dedicated bank and strict customer tag, then asks Hindsight `reflect` for a structured, concise handoff. It does not send the history of every customer.

## 2:15–2:40 — Show retrieved history

Read the current situation and previous discussions. Open **Customer memory** to show recalled facts attributed to Employee A, with timestamps and conversation provenance.

## 2:40–3:00 — Surface uncertainty

Point to the **Open / unconfirmed** warning: the 20% discount was discussed but not approved. The possible purchase next month remains a possible next step, not a definite sale.

Close with: **“Person A learned something from Rahul. Later, Person B knows it without Rahul having to repeat himself.”**
